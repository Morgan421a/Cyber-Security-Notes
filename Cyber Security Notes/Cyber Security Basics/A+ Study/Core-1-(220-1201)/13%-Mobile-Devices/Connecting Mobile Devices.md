- Wired and Wireless connectivity
	- Standards and options change throughout the years
- Connections often used for Synchronisation, connectivity, backup, and identification
	- Sometimes at the same time

### USB
- **Universal Serial Bus (USB)**
	- High-Speed wired communication
	- Used for charging and data transfers
- Different types such as:
	- **Micro B Plug** <- popular for older devices, occasionally still used today
	- **Mini B Plug**
	- **Type A Plug** <- Typically still used to connect to devices such as desktop and laptop computers
##### USB-C
- Typically used for modern devices
- 24-pin double-sided USB connector
	- Used for both hosts and devices
- Acts as a USB 2.0/3.0/3.1/4 connection
	- Different signals can use the same connector
- Can transmits other signals such as:
	- Display Port
	- HDMI
	- Thunderbolt
- Designed to be easy to use (can be plugged in in any orientation)
- Supports higher speed than older style USB connectors
##### Lightning
- Apple proprietary
- 8-Pin digital signals
- Used for iPhone and iPad devices (model and country dependent, some now use USB-C)
- Has some advantages over Micro-USB such as:
	- Higher power output for phones and tablets (Faster Charging)
	- Can be inserted in either way (similar to USB-C)
	- Simpler design (Supported across most of Apple's mobile devices)

### NFC
- Near Field Communication (NFC)
	- Used to send small amounts of data wirelessly over a limited area (typically 4 cm or less)
- Built into devices such as phones and smart watches
- Often used for:
	- Payment Systems
	- Transportation cards/ID
	- In-person information exchange
- Some orgs may use access tokens/identity "cards" for authentication and access to certain systems (usually by tapping them on a wall sensor)
	- Good due to NFC's short range and encryption support

### Bluetooth
- High speed communication over short distances
	- Typically called a **Personal Area Network (PAN)**
- Commonly used to connect mobile devices:
	- **Smartphones**
	- **Tethering** <- Sharing a device's internet connection with another device via the Bluetooth PAN instead of a WiFi hotspot
	- **Wireless Headsets and headphones**
	- **Health monitors**
	- **Automobile and phone integration**
	- **Smartwatches**
	- **External Speakers**
##### Hotspot/Tethering
- Mobile phones can be used to connect other devices to the internet, akin to a **router**
- **Hotspot** = **Multiple devices** connecting to a mobile phone for internet access
- **Tethering** = **One device**, and only that one device connecting to a mobile phone for internet access
- Dependent on phone type, software and provider
	- May incur additional charges and data costs
