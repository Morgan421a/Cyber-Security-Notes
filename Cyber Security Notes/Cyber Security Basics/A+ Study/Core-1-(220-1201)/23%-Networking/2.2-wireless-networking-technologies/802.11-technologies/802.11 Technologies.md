- Wireless networking standards fall under the IEEE 802.11 family
	- Defines frequency bands, speeds, and compatibility to ensure efficient connectivity

#### 802.11a
- **Frequency band** = **5 GHz**
- **Max Speed** = **54 Mbps**
- Limited to business environments due to high costs of 5 GHz radios

#### 802.11b
- **Frequency Band** = **2.4 GHz**
- **Max speed** = **11 Mbps**
- Affordable, widely adopted for homes, schools, and businesses
- **Prone to interference** from household devices, such as microwaves and Bluetooth

#### 802.11g
- **Frequency Band** = **2.4 GHz**
- **Max speed** = **54 Mbps**
- Backward compatible with 802.11b devices

#### 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4)
- **Frequency Bands** = **2.4 GHz** and **5 GHz** (**Dual-band**)
- **Max speed** = **300 - 600 Mbps**
- Introduced **MIMO** (Multiple Input Multiple Output) **technology for improved throughput**
- Backward compatible with 802.11b and 802.11g devices

#### 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5)
- **Frequency Band** = **5 GHz**
- **Max Speed** = **Theoretical 6.9 Gbps**, **Typically around 1 Gbps** in real-world conditions
- **MU-MIMO** (Multi-User MIMO) for **simultaneous multi-device communication**
- **No native support for 2.4 GHz** 
	- **dual-radio access points** tend to **include** it **for backward compatibility**

#### 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6 / Wi-Fi 6E)
- **Frequency Bands**:
	- **Wi-Fi 6** = **2.4 GHz** and **5 GHz** (**Dual-Band**)
	- **Wi-Fi 6E** = **2.4 GHz**, **5 GHz**, and **6 GHz** (**Tri-Band**)
- **Max Speed** = **Theoretical 9.6 Gbps**
- **MU-MIMO and OF DMA** (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access) **for better efficiency**
- **6 GHz band adds** up to **59 non-overlapping channels for reduced congestion**
