- Uses **Optical cables/Fibre optic cables/Fibre Cables**
### Fibre Communication
- **Transmission by light**
	- The Visible Spectrum
- No Radio Frequency (RF) signal
	- Difficult to monitor or tap
	- **Immune to Radio Interference**
- **Signal degrades** much **slower** than copper
	- Allows for **transmission over long distances**
- Consists of thin strands of glass or plastic
- **Immune to Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)**
- Boasts **high speeds** <- Can handle **terabits** or **petabits** **per second**
- **More expensive** than copper for **materials** and **installation**
	- Requires precision tools and expertise
	- More difficult to terminate and repair
- Transmission:
	1. Light ray sent into cable, typically via an **LED** or **Laser**
	2. Light moves through fibre optic on the **core** of the fibre, often called a **High Reflective Index core**
		- **Core** Surrounded by a **low reflective index cladding** to protect the light
	3. Light bounces between the different reflective indexes until it reaches a receiver on the other side
		- Fibre is thin and very fragile, as such is surrounded with a **buffer coating** to protect it

### Multimode Fibre (MMF)
- Commonly used inside of **buildings** and for **short range communication**
	- **Usually up to 2 km** but **can vary**
- Due to **short travel distance**, a relatively **inexpensive light source** such as **LED** can be used
- Called **multimode** fibre because, due to the size of the fibre the **light can take different modes (stable paths)** as it travels through the core
	- each time light travels through, it may take a different path to reach the other end
- Usually Identified by **aqua blue or orange sheaths**

### Single-mode Fibre (SMF)
- Designed for **Long-range** communication
	- **Up to 100 km without processing** (needing to regenerate the signal)
- Needs an **expensive light source**
	- **Commonly** uses **lasers**
- **Light travels** through the fibre in a **single mode (path)**
	- Core much narrower than multimode fibre
- **Typically** Identified by a **yellow sheath**
