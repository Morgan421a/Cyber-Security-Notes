### Case Fans
- **Cool air pulled through a PC**
	- **Hot air** usually **expelled through opposite side of case**
	- Always **check for good airflow** inside case
- **Motherboard** **and component layout important**
	- Both contribute to how well air circulates through case
	- Wires, devices and Power supply units should be out of the way to ensure a good path for air to flow
- **Different types of cases** **and cooling systems** in size, style, and volume levels

### On-board Fans
- **Fans built onto** an **adaptor card**
	- Designed to cool the entire adaptor card
- Takes cool air from computer and directs it to a single point on the adaptor card
- Can be bulky and may take additional adaptor card space
- Typically seen on high-end graphics cards or video cards

### Fan Specifications
- **Standard Sizes**:
	- 80 mm, 120 mm, 200 mm, etc.
- **Different Speeds**
	- Fans often have variable speed <- Run slower when computer is cool, run faster when it gets hotter
- **Different noise levels**
	- Not all fans sound the same, some are designed to be quieter
	- The faster a fan spins the more noise it makes
- Fans **should be cleaned every 3 to 6 months to remove dust**, which can damage them, **and maintain airflow efficiency**

### Fanless / Passive Cooling
- No Fans = No Noise
	- **Silent Operation**
- Typically used for **devices in areas where low noise** is **desired**, such as for video servers, TV set top boxes satellite receiver, media servers, etc.
- **Usually** used **in purpose-built devices/appliances** **where** the **amount of heat can be managed easily**
	- Often using **low-power components**
- **Functions** are **very controlled through carefully measured thermal tests**
- Used alongside a **Heat Sink** to take heat from individual components and spread it over a larger area

### Heat Sinks
- **Dissipate Heat through thermal conduction**
	- Often **copper or aluminium alloy construction**
- **Spreads heat from** a **component across** many **fins/grids**, **increasing** the **surface area for cooling**
	- **Heat** then **transferred** **to** the **cooler air**
- **Thermal Paste** creates a contact between the chip and the heat sink
- Get very hot, caution should be taken when touching them if they've been used

### Thermal Paste
- Sometimes called **Thermal Grease** or **Conductive Grease**
- A **Thermally conductive adhesive**
- **Placed between** the **heat sink and component** 
	- **Improve thermal conductivity** **by moving heat away from the component and removing air gaps between processor and heat sink** 
- Often only needs a small amount
	- About the size of a pea
- Not re-usable

### Thermal Pad
- Conducts heat without the mess risk of thermal paste
	- Cut to size and install on component
- **Easy to use** and **won't leak** and damage component
- **Almost as effective as thermal paste**, but **not quite as good**
- Not re-usable
	- Must be replaced once removed

### Liquid Cooling
- Coolant Circulated through a computer
- Often found in **high-end systems** but **popularity for gaming, graphics, and overclocking focused computers is rising**
- **More efficient and quieter** than cooling with fans
- Types of liquid cooling systems:
	- **Closed loop systems**
		- Self-contained systems cooling a single component
		- Includes a heat sink, radiator and liquid coolant
	- **Open Loop Systems**
		- Customisable systems capable of cooling multiple components
		- Includes **multiple components**:
			- **Water loop/tubing** - Circulates coolant throughout the system
			- **Pump** - Pushes Liquid through the system
			- **Reservoir** - Holds coolant, accommodating expansion and contraction
			- **Water Block/Bracket** - Transfers heat from components to the liquid
			- **Radiator** - Dissipates heat from coolant as air passes over its surface
		- Operates in a continuous cycle to maintain optimal temperatures
