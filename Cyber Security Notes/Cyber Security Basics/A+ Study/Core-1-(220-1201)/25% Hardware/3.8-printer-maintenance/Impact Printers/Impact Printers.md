
### Dot-Matrix Printers
- Common **type of impact printer**
- **Print head has small matrix of pins**
	- **Pins strike an ink ribbon to make a mark on paper**, similar to a typewriter
- Only used in relatively niche cases
- Commonly used in industries that require receipts and documentation with signatures on multiple copies, such as:
	- Garages, logistics, and healthcare
- **Advantages**:
	- Can print carbon copies/multiple copies of a page (Multi-part forms)
	- Relatively low cost per page, ribbon cartridges inexpensive and long lasting
	- Durable, able to operate in harsh environments
	- Requires minimal maintenance compared to other printer types
- **Disadvantages**:
	- Noisy operation
	- Poor print quality <- effectively limited to text-based applications
	- Slower speed due to printing process

### Dot-Matrix Printer head
- Printer head moves back and forth across page
	- Pins hit ink ribbon and paper
- One matrix of pins, meaning head must move across the page to print
- Commonly have a heat sink on the back
	- Tend to generate a decent amount of heat
- Print **resolution dependent on number of pins in printhead**
	- **Typically 100 to 240 DPI** <- Much lower than inkjet and laser printers
- **Platen** = rubber roller that helps press paper against printhead to ensure consistent pressure and print quality

### Printer Ribbon
- Typically a single cartridge that fits inside the printer
	-  **Ribbon runs through cartridge in a circle/loop**
	- Made of **fabric or another material** that's **saturated with ink**
- Ribbon usually stretches across entire page, making it easier for printhead to move back and forth for the output
- Easy to replace
- **Cartridge size** usually **proprietary**
	- Cartridge must be designed for specific printer make and model

### Tractor Feed
- Continuously feeds paper through the printer
	- Paper often has perforations so tractor feed holes can be removed after printing
- **Paper pulled through with holes** **on** either sides of **paper**
	- **Instead of using friction**
	- **Ensures accurate alignment for multi-part forms and reduces misalignment issues**
- Paper typically called **Tractor Paper**
- **Green bar tractor paper** **was** **commonly used** in IT **to output source code**

### Multipart Paper
- Carbonless copying
	- **Multiple copies of** a **single print in** a **single pass**
- Impact with top sheet duplicated on pages underneath
	- **Micro-encapsulated ink** on **back of** the **first sheet**
	- **Clay surface** on **top of second sheet** reacts to create copy underneath
- **Inks, dyes, and clay on paper can cause mild irritation with certain chemical combinations**
- **Easier and less expensive to just print another copy**
	- Rare to find multipart paper being used
