### Laser Printer
- A **Printer** that **combines** a **laser, high voltage, charged ions, powdered ink, heat, and paper**
- **Advantages**:
	- Produces Very High quality outputs
	- Fast printing speeds
- **Disadvantages**:
	- Very Complex <- Many moving parts, requires on-printer memory and processing
	- If ink leaks out of cartridge will cause a lot of mess

### Laser Printer Parts
- **Imaging Drum/OPC Drum**:
	- Photosensitive drum that hold an electrostatic charge to create the image
	- Receives a negative charge from the primary charge roller or corona wire
- **Fuser Assembly**:
	- Permanently bonds toner to paper using heat and pressure
	- Uses a heated roller and a pressure roller
- **Transfer Belt/Roller**:
	- Applies positive charge to pull toner from drum onto the paper
	- Ensures even toner application prior to fusing
- **Pickup Rollers**:
	- Pull single sheet of paper from feed tray
	- Prevents multiple sheets from being picked up at once
- **Paper Separation Pad**:
	- Prevents multiple sheets from entering the feed path
	- Reduces paper jams and ensures proper alignment
- **Duplex Assembly** (If applicable):
	- Flips paper for double sided printing
	- Reduces paper jams and ensures proper alignment

### Laser Printer Process
1. **Corona wire or charge roller** negatively charges the drum
2. The **laser** neutralises the negative charge <- Writes the image of print onto the outside of the photosensitive drum
3. **Negatively charged toner** (ink) added to the photosensitive drum, toner **only** sticks to areas marked by the laser since both **toner and unmarked parts of drum** are **both negatively charged**
4. **Paper** passed next to drum and toner on drum transferred to the paper
5. Paper passes through the **Fuser** which uses **heat and pressure** to melt toner to the page
6. Remaining toner on **drum is cleaned off** for another pass

### Replacing the Toner Cartridge
- **Toner (ink)** = Takes image from memory of printer and represents it on a page
- Running low on toner produces lighter and lighter results until eventually nothing is printed on the paper
	- Low doesn't mean empty
- **Toner can contain the OPC Drum**
	- **OPC Drum** = The photosensitive drum
	- **Sensitive to light** <- Should be kept in packaging bag until being installed
	- **Toner cartridges typically last for ~10,000 printed pages**
- **Typically lasts for ~2500 printed pages**
- **Cartridge changing process**:
	1. Power of Printer for safety
	2. Lift cover of printer
		- Some cartridges kept in top of printer, some kept in the side, some have multiple for different colours
	3. Remove old cartridge
	4. Remove packing strips or packing materials from new cartridge
	5. Insert new cartridge
	6. Restart printer

### Laser Printer Maintenance Kit
- **Overtime** the **moving parts in** a **laser printer** will **wear out** **due to** the constant **movement** as well as the **heat and pressure**
- **Manufacturers make standard maintenance kits** to **replace** the **parts** that **commonly** **wear out**, including parts such as:
	- Replacement feed rollers, new fuser unit, etc.
- A printer's **page counter** can be **used to indicate when** the **printer may need maintenance**
	- **Most manufacturers have** a **recommended page count** **before** **maintenance** kit will likely need to be used
- **Printer MUST be powered down first**, only after can the components be replaced
	- **If** **Printer** has been **used recently** **DO NOT touch fuser unit as they become very hot**
- **After maintenance kit** has been **installed**, **reset the page counter** for ease of monitoring until next maintenance is needed

### Laser Printer Calibration
- **Different toner cartridges** **print** with **different densities**, some dark, some light
	- **Too much toner or Too little toner**
- **Calibration** can be **used to adjust** the **density**
- **Calibration** can be **automated** in the printer **or** a **manual process**
	- Manual requires checking a printed page, changing the print density, then checking again
	- **Calibration type should be listed in the printer manual**

### Laser Printer Cleaning
- Laser printers **get very dirty due to** the **toner and paper dust accumulating** within them
	- Toner should be handled properly to reduce risk of spilling inside printer or in the air <- toner = very fine dust, can spread in the air very easily
- **Manufacturer's recommendations should be checked prior to cleaning**
	- Check what substances can be used <- **Typically water or isopropyl alcohol** (IPA)
	- **DO NOT USE HARSH CHEMICALS**
- **Exterior** typically recommended to be cleaned using a **damp cloth**
	- **Most often** damp with **just water**
- **Interior** to **dust**  **should be wiped away** 
	- **Don't use Compressed Air** <- Will spread toner and paper dust into surrounding air
	- **Don't use a normal vacuum cleaner, only ones specially designed to vacuum toner**
	- **Components** such as rollers typically cleaned using **cold water or IPA**
		- **Check manufacturer's guide first**
	- **If toner gets on skin**:
		- Use only **cold water to wash** it **off**
			- **DO NOT USE WARM OR HOT WATER** <- Could melt toner, making it harder to remove

### Laser Printer Common Troubleshooting Issues
- **Incomplete Prints** <- Typically **printer memory** needs to be **upgraded**
- **Paper Jams** <- Check and **clean pickup rollers and separation pad**
- **Faded Prints** <- **Replace toner cartridge**
- **Ghosting** <- **Replace Drum unit or Fuser Assembly**
- Smudging <- **Check** and **replace fuser assembly**
