### Thermal Printer
- Uses **white paper that turns black when heated**
	- Heat only applied to areas of paper in order to output image from memory
- **Commonly used for** 
	- Retail and hospitality receipts
	- Shipping and barcode labels
	- Producing ID badges and wristbands
- **Often found in industries such as**:
	- Retail, healthcare, and logistics industries
- **Speed measured in inches or centimetres per second** instead of Pages Per Minute (PPM)
- **Advantages**:
	- Reliable
	- Low maintenance requirements <- Fewer moving parts
	- **No ink** 
	- Very quiet <- Near silent
	- **Fast and efficient** printing **for specialised tasks**
	- Often compact and portable
- **Disadvantages**:
	- Paper sensitive to light and heat
		- Some clear tapes can cause a chemical reaction with the paper, turning the page white
	- Prints prone to fading and damage from friction
		- Unsuitable for long-term document storage
	- Print quality has limited resolution compared to inkjet and laser printers
		- **Resolution typically between 100 and 300 DPI**

### Types of Thermal Printers
- **Direct Thermal Printer**:
	- Uses thermal paper that reacts to heat
	- No ink or ribbon needed
	- Print fades overtime
	- Print sensitive to light and heat
	- Commonly used for receipts, labels, and barcodes
- **Thermal Transfer Printer**:
	- Use thermal ribbon to transfer ink from ribbon onto regular paper
	- Durable, high quality prints
	- Suitable for long-lasting labels and high quality images
	- Commonly used for product labels, ID badges, and asset tags

### Thermal Printer Parts
- **Feed Assembly**:
	- Pulls paper through the printer
	- Friction based mechanism to guide paper under printhead
- **Heating Element** (printhead):
	- Located in the printhead
	- Heats up specific areas to create images or text
	- **Determines print resolution**, measured in **Dots Per Inch** (DPI)
	- Extends across entire length of the printing area
	- No moving printhead
	- Commonly a single piece
- **Thermal Paper or Ribbon**:
	- **Direct Thermal Printers** use **heat sensitive paper**
	- **Thermal transfer printers** use a **ribbon that transfers ink onto paper** (ink on ribbon, not in printer itself)
	- **CMYK ribbons** used in **colour thermal printers**

### Thermal Paper
- Thermal Paper = **Thermochromic Paper** / **Thermal Paper**
- **Paper covered with** **chemical** **that changes colour when heated**
- Commonly seen used in tills, credit card terminals, label printers for mail, etc.
- Has a noticeably different texture compared to normal paper and looks slightly glossy
